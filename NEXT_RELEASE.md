**Identify this chord** now reads a chord note's name. A note made from a template, with a name like "A Minor 7 - Open" and empty properties, gets its chord and its shape in one click.

#### Identify this chord

- **From the note's name**: on a chord note with no chord symbol, no frets and no pitches, the command reads the note's name. "A Minor 7 - Open" gives the symbol Am7, its root, quality and alias, and the shape Open (#119).
- **One click on guitar and piano**: the click window opens, with the name's chord above the drawing ("The note's name says A Minor 7 (Am7), with the shape Open.") and the button **Name it Am7, from the note's name**. The button has the focus, so Enter names the note too. You can still click the notes instead: the chord and the frets or keys are written, and the name's shape too when they make the name's chord. Other instruments have no click window: there, the name applies at once.
- **The names it reads**: the chord alone ("Am7", "A Minor 7"), the chord, a dash and the shape ("A Minor 7 - Open"), or the shape in brackets when the note type lists it ("A Minor 7 (Open)"). The chord can be a symbol or words, in any case, with its root stuck to its quality too ("AMinor 7 - Open"). The type's prefix and suffix are left out ("A Minor 7 - Open (Guitar Chord)").
- **What it leaves alone**: the shape is written only when the note type has the shape property and the note has no shape yet. Frets or pitches on the note come first: then the name isn't read. Nothing is renamed.

#### Other changes

- **In French too**: the new texts are translated. The button reads "Nommer Am7, d'après le nom de la note".
